What is the ED?

The Directorate of Enforcement (ED) is India's financial investigation agency, working under the Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance. Set up in 1956, it mainly enforces the laws on money laundering and foreign exchange. Its headquarters is Pravartan Bhawan in New Delhi, with zonal offices across the country.

Which laws does the ED enforce?
  • PMLA, 2002 – Prevention of Money Laundering Act
  • FEMA, 1999 – Foreign Exchange Management Act
  • FEOA, 2018 – Fugitive Economic Offenders Act
How does an ED case usually move?
  • An ECIR is registered, based on an FIR for the underlying crime (by police, CBI, etc.)
  • Searches and seizures
  • Summons for questioning; arrest where needed
  • Provisional attachment of property
  • Prosecution complaint filed before a special PMLA court

Glossary

ECIR – Enforcement Case Information Report; the ED's case record, similar to an FIR.
PAO – Provisional Attachment Order; temporarily freezes property.
PC – Prosecution Complaint; the ED's equivalent of a chargesheet, filed in a special court.
PoC – Proceeds of Crime; money or property obtained through crime.
Hawala – An informal network for moving money outside the banking system.

Remember: a raid, summons or arrest does not mean guilt is proven. Charges are decided in court.

⚠️ Beware of fake ED summons! Fraudsters pose as ED officers to demand money. If you receive a summons, check it in the "Verify Your Summons" section of the ED's official website.
